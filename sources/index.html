Where our advice comes from

Every verdict rule in KitchenScan is based on guidance from a food-safety agency, a consumer group or the maker. These are the sources, with our own short summary of each.

Sources

Singapore Food Agency

Safe Practices for Non-Stick Pans

Replace non-stick cookware once the coating visibly deteriorates: excessive scratches, peeling, flaking or chipping. Use wooden, silicone or plastic utensils and medium or low heat.

Consumer Reports

Make Your Nonstick Frying Pan Last

A nonstick pan that is scratched or flaking is due for replacement; flakes can end up in food.

Lodge Cast Iron

Restore and Season Rusty Cast Iron

Rust on cast iron can be scoured off; re-season the pan in the oven to restore it.

All-Clad

Use & Care

Heat tints and small white pits on stainless steel are cosmetic and do not affect cooking.

Le Creuset

Care and Use

Stop using a piece whose interior enamel has chipped.

Philips

The coating of my Philips Airfryer's pan or basket peels off

Peeling comes from scratching the coating; avoid metal scrubbers and hard brushes.

USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service

Cutting Boards

Discard cutting boards that become excessively worn or develop hard-to-clean grooves.

Harvard Health Publishing

Microwaving food in plastic: Dangerous or not?

Only containers that passed FDA review may carry a microwave-safe label; use those for heating.

Make Magazine

Ask CRAFT: Rusty baking pan

Light rust on uncoated bakeware can be scrubbed off before baking again.
